2. Pedagogy & Instructional Methodologies
2. Pedagogy & Instructional Methodologies 4 Questions
Q4. Lesson Plan Construction & Pacing 5 Mins
"Walk me through how you construct a standard lesson plan."
What to Look For: Backward design (beginning with the learning objective/assessment), clear pacing, differentiation.
Ideal Answer: "I start with a clear, measurable learning objective (SWBAT). I structure the lesson using the 'I Do, We Do, You Do' gradual release model: a 5-minute hook/warm-up, direct instruction, guided group practice, independent practice, and a 5-minute exit ticket for assessment."
Q5. 10-Minute Model Lesson Execution 5 Mins
"Can you walk me through a 10-minute model lesson you’ve recently taught?"
What to Look For: Active engagement, strong time management, clear learning outcome.
Ideal Answer: "I taught a 10-minute micro-lesson on identifying rhetorical devices in persuasive text. I introduced 'Ethos, Pathos, and Logos' with three short ads, had students categorize quotes in pairs using whiteboards, and ended with a brief quick-write exercise."
Q6. Integration of VARK Learning Modalities 4 Mins
"How do you implement VARK learning styles in an English classroom?"
What to Look For: Multi-sensory lesson design without over-complicating daily delivery.
Ideal Answer: "I blend modalities into every unit. For a novel study, Visual learners get graphic organizers and character maps; Auditory learners engage in Socratic seminars and audiobooks; Read/Write learners analyze text passages; and Kinesthetic learners do role-plays or interactive station activities."
Q7. Differentiated Instruction for Mixed Abilities 5 Mins
"How do you handle differentiation for high-achievers vs. struggling readers?"
What to Look For: Tiered assignments, scaffolded reading tools, extension tasks.
Ideal Answer: "I scaffold reading for struggling students using graphic organizers, vocabulary banks, and chunked texts. For high-achievers, I elevate the depth of complexity rather than giving extra work—such as asking them to critique authorial choices or evaluate alternative perspectives."
3. EdTech & Frameworks (TPACK)
3. EdTech & Frameworks (TPACK) 2 Questions
Q8. Understanding and Application of TPACK 4 Mins
"What is your understanding of the TPACK framework, and how do you apply it?"
What to Look For: Understanding that technology supports pedagogy and content, rather than replacing it.
Ideal Answer: "TPACK is the intersection of Technological, Pedagogical, and Content Knowledge. I don't use tech for its own sake. I pick tools that enhance content delivery—for example, using interactive annotation software (Tech) during close reading (Pedagogy) of a Shakespearean text (Content)."
Q9. Everyday Educational Technology Stack 4 Mins
"What EdTech tools do you use daily to drive English engagement?"
What to Look For: Practical, modern toolset (Google Classroom, Quizizz, Padlet, Canvas, NoRedInk, Turnitin, AI literacy tools).
Ideal Answer: "I use Google Classroom for assignment workflow, Padlet for interactive brainstorming, NoRedInk for targeted grammar drills, and Quizizz for quick formative vocabulary assessments."
4. Specialized Test Prep (SAT, IGCSE, GAT, SAAT)
4. Specialized Test Prep (SAT, IGCSE, GAT, SAAT) 3 Questions
Q10. IGCSE English Language & Literature Strategy 5 Mins
"How do you approach teaching IGCSE English Language and Literature?"
What to Look For: Knowledge of assessment objectives (AO1–AO4), marking schemes, paper structures, and time management strategies.
Ideal Answer: "I focus heavily on command words and mark scheme criteria. For Language, we train on text analysis, writer’s effect, and summary skills under timed conditions. For Literature, I teach PEEL paragraph structures and detailed quotation analysis."
Q11. SAT Digital Reading & Writing Preparation 5 Mins
"What is your strategy for training students on SAT Digital Reading & Writing?"
What to Look For: Familiarity with question types (Craft & Structure, Information & Ideas, Expression of Ideas, Standard English Conventions) and pacing.
Ideal Answer: "I break the test down by question patterns and logic rules. We focus on active reading strategies, context clues for vocabulary questions, grammar rules for mechanics, and process of elimination to increase speed and precision."
Q12. Local Aptitude & Achievement Tests (GAT / SAAT) 4 Mins
"How do you prepare local students for English-based aptitude/achievement tests like GAT and SAAT?"
What to Look For: Focus on academic vocabulary, sentence completion, reading speed, and high-frequency root words.
Ideal Answer: "I build explicit root-word and context-clue vocabulary modules, along with reading compression techniques tailored to standardized multiple-choice formats, helping students improve both speed and accuracy."
5. Classroom Management & Student Dynamics
5. Classroom Management & Student Dynamics 2 Questions
Q13. Discipline Philosophy & Proactive Management 4 Mins
"What is your classroom management philosophy when dealing with disruptive students?"
What to Look For: Proactive routines, clear expectations, non-confrontational discipline stairs (praise in public, correct in private).
Ideal Answer: "I set clear expectations and routines on day one. When disruptions occur, I use non-verbal cues or proximity first. If needed, I address the student privately to understand the root cause before applying clear, consistent consequences."
Q14. Re-Engaging Unmotivated Secondary Students 4 Mins
"How do you re-engage an unmotivated or difficult Grade 9–12 student?"
What to Look For: Building rapport, choice-based learning, relevant text selection.
Ideal Answer: "I find out what interests them outside class and connect English concepts to those topics. I also offer choices in assignment formats when possible and provide small, achievable milestones to rebuild their confidence."

6. Workload, Co-Curriculars & Dedication 2 Questions
Q15. Heavy Marking Workload & Burnout Prevention 4 Mins
"Teaching Grades 4–12 involves heavy marking loads. How do you handle workload and stress?"
What to Look For: Time management, rubric-based grading, peer review, sustainable habits.
Ideal Answer: "I rely on clear rubrics, targeted feedback focus (concentrating on 1–2 key skills per essay), and peer-editing rotations. I batch-grade during planning blocks to maintain quick turnaround times without burning out."
Q16. Co-Curricular Contributions at Alrowad 3 Mins
"What co-curricular activities can you lead or support at Alrowad?"
What to Look For: Willingness to contribute beyond the classroom (Debate Club, School Newspaper, Model UN, Drama/Literary Magazine).
Ideal Answer: "I’m well-prepared to run a Debate Club or Model UN (MUN) program to build public speaking skills, or direct a creative writing workshop/literary journal."
